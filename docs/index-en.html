XyGrib for Android

Sailors have long used the desktop program XyGrib (formerly zyGrib) on Windows, Linux and macOS. It has no mobile version, and people have been asking for one on sailing forums for years.

MAKGrib is a fork of XyGrib brought to the phone: the same GRIB engine and the same chart, with a screen rebuilt for a finger and a moving deck. Desktop builds exist too — it is one program.

What it does

It downloads forecasts straight from the American weather service NOAA for the patch of sea on your screen, not for half the world. The file stays on the device and opens again next time you start: at sea there may be no connection for a week, and that is the point of the program.

  • wind and gusts — barbed arrows over a colour field;
  • waves — height, direction and period;
  • pressure — isobars over any layer;
  • air and sea temperature;
  • fog — temperature minus dew point;
  • cloud, rain, thunderstorms.

Route and time of arrival

Tap the chart to lay a route, drag the points to adjust. The app computes the distance along great circles, the passage time at your speed and the hour of arrival. The vessel moves along the route as you step through the forecast, so you see which wind it will meet and where.

Weather at a point

Hold your finger on the chart and a small window shows the numbers for that hour: wind and direction, gusts, wave, pressure, air and sea temperature, cloud and rain.

NOAA's wave model does not compute enclosed basins. Waves for the Caspian, Black and Azov seas will come from the German weather service, whose model does cover them — measured, not assumed.

How it differs from Windy and the rest

Windy is excellent while you have a connection. At sea you do not. MAKGrib keeps the downloaded forecast on the device and shows it without a network — that is the difference between checking the weather and working with a forecast.

No subscription, no account, no advertising, no data collection. The source is open, so the program cannot be closed down or quietly turned into a paid service over its users' heads.

Questions

What does it cost?

Nothing. No paid features, no subscription, no ads.

Do I need internet?

Only to download a forecast. After that it lives on the device.

How big is a forecast?

Usually 200 KB to 1 MB, depending on the area, how many days and the time step.

Can I navigate by it?

No. It is a weather forecast and an aid to planning a passage, not a navigation instrument and not a substitute for official warnings.
